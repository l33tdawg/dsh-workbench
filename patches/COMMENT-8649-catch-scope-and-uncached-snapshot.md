Thanks — that is the front half of the gate, and it lands in the record I keep for this defect: `dsh-skill/lib/index.js:346-356` in the 0.2.0-rc.2 bundle, the same catch that finding attributes `cacheable = false` to (`:349-356`), which I had cited without quoting the loop around it. Two details in your last paragraph are worth stating precisely, because one of them moves *who* drops *what*.

**The catch is per provider, not per layer.** The `try` at `:349` wraps one `provider.list()` inside the loop at `:346`. A provider that throws leaves `output === undefined`, so the `continue` at `:356` skips only that provider: the candidates the other providers already pushed, and the layer's own runtime skills pushed at `:336-345`, all survive into the return at `:371-374`. What your sentence is right about is the outcome, and the mechanism is one layer up rather than in the catch. `collectFresh` carries `cacheable = false` into the snapshot (`:302-306`), and gate 2 then returns before the catalog message is built (`dsh-tool-skill/lib/index.js:216`), so the entries that did survive are collected and never published. The throwing provider contributes nothing *and* nobody else's contribution reaches the model — total loss from a partial failure, which is the part that deserves the per-root guard.

**The uncached snapshot is exactly as you describe, and it is the useful half.** `collect()` writes `collectCache` only under `if (result.cacheable)` (`dsh-skill/lib/index.js:288-289`), and the hit branch returns `{ entries: cached, cacheable: true }` (`:272-275`), so `cacheable` is doing double duty as "complete": a cached snapshot is complete by construction, and an incomplete one is rediscovered on every request. Your diagnostic reading holds — a repeated `skipped` line in the log means the provider is still throwing, and in this profile it is the bigint `stat` in the archive root rather than an intermittent failure.

Both are now in the finding I keep, since neither was: the loop that scopes the loss, and the constant rediscovery as a signal. One note for the visibility half, because the `warn` at `:354` does fire: it goes through `ctx.logger`, and what this profile does with that is its own open report — [2905](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness/discussions/2905), whose title ends "`ctx.logger` has no sink in any shipped profile".
